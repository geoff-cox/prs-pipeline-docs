# Exercise 2 — Blind Pass, Then Recode

**Prerequisites:** Exercise 1 reviewed and approved by [PI NAME]; Handbook Part I read closely.
**Materials:** a batch of [BATCH SIZE] real responses to the pivots question, supplied by [PI NAME] via the shared folder, plus `templates/blind-pass-sheet.csv`.
**Time (assuming a 15–20 response batch):** Part A ~2–3 hours; Part B ~1.5–2 hours (after clearance). **Strictly independent throughout** — no comparing with the other coder until the debrief meeting.

## Why this exercise is one-shot

You currently do *not* know the project's codebook. That makes you something the project can never get back once spent: a careful, fresh reader. Part A measures whether the codebook's categories are ones that fresh readers *discover on their own* — which is evidence about the codebook itself, not about you. This is why the reading restrictions in the orientation exist, and why Part A comes before you open the sealed companion.

## Part A — Blind pass

For each response in your batch, fill one row per identified episode (multiple episodes → multiple rows) in the blind-pass sheet:

| Column | What to enter |
|--------|---------------|
| `response_id` | Copy exactly. |
| `episode_n` | 1, 2, … within the response. |
| `own_words_rule` | **The heart of the exercise.** One sentence: *"The student decides ___ because ___."* Capture the rule/criterion the student appears to be using, in your own plain words. |
| `my_category` | A short label **you invent** for the *kind* of rule this is (e.g., "counting-things rule"). Reuse your own labels across responses when the same kind recurs — consistency of *your* labels is the point. Keep a personal legend. |
| `criterion_verbatim` / `judgment_verbatim` | As in Handbook Part I. |
| `complete` | 1/0 per Handbook Part I. |
| `c1` / `c2` | Two-digit code for complete episodes. |
| `relation` | chained(k) / independent / blank. |
| `oov_note` | Off-standard terminology, verbatim. |
| `confidence` | 3/2/1. |
| `notes` | Alternatives, doubts, anything. |

Also keep a running **category legend** (a second sheet tab): each `my_category` label with a one-line definition and a count of uses. Expect to revise it as you go — that's fine; just keep it current.

**Rules of the road:** Handbook Part I edge cases apply. Blanks and bare answers still get a row. Do not look anything up in the textbook beyond the question itself. Do not discuss responses with the other coder. Rules questions go to [PI NAME].

**Submit:** the sheet + legend to [PI NAME]. Then stop and wait for clearance.

## Part B — Recode with the codebook (after clearance)

1. Read the sealed companion (`02-codebook-companion-SEALED.md`) and the codebook CSV. Twice.
2. Return to your Part A sheet — **do not alter your Part A columns** — and fill the `code` column for every episode per the companion's recode rules (single fit / multiple fit with `ALT:` / `NONE` / squint-fit).
3. Write the **comparison memo** (½–1 page): your invented categories vs. the CS codes — clean mappings, convergences, codes that never appeared, and everything you coded `NONE`. End with one paragraph: what would you change about the codebook, and why?

**Submit:** updated sheet + memo before the debrief meeting on [DATE].

## What happens at the debrief

We put both coders' sheets side by side, look at where you agreed and disagreed *before ever discussing*, and mine your `NONE`s and legends for codebook and vocabulary revisions. Nothing about disagreement is bad — measured disagreement is literally a publishable quantity.
